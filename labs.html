our labs

Four interlinked ways of knowing the city.

A living ecosystem where researching, learning, making and reflecting coexist. At a pace that suits them, it invites practitioners, researchers and community members to experiment with forms of knowing that transcend text.

01
01 — urban sonic lab

Urban Sonic Lab

Exploring the soundscapes of the city as data, archive and method. It listens to the rhythms of everyday life, tracing how movement, noise, silence and memory shape belonging and urban identity.

02
02 — ground works

Ground Works

It reclaims the ground as a site of knowledge, collaboration and resistance. The lab works through public art, community co-design and creative infrastructure-building, drawing together communities, artisans, artists and urban designers to reimagine.

03
03 — ai commons

AI Commons

A shared space for experimenting with artificial intelligence as a public tool. It seeks to decentralise digital knowledge, using AI to amplify local narratives, collective memory and community-led problem-solving rather than extract from them.

04
04 — fabric of society

Fabric of Society

Exploring textiles, craft and material culture as social infrastructure. This lab threads together making and meaning, reflecting on how fabric, both literal and symbolic, holds histories of migration, care and collaboration across generations.

method of engagement

Trust, reciprocity and shared care.

Together, the four labs form an equitable creative urban community of practice — one that blends ancestral knowledge with speculative futures to reimagine how cities are lived, built and understood. Nobody is obligated, and the model is intentionally fluid, allowing people to enter, contribute and depart in ways that honour both personal rhythm and community flow. Lab programming operates on a minimum two-year cycle, shaped by community needs rather than temporary funding priorities.

Open lab hours invite the public to explore, experiment and engage with ongoing work one day a month. Project hours are reserved for focused collaboration within specific labs by members. All members can access the co-working space and Library of Things — borrowing tools such as field recorders, cameras or dye kits — and can participate in workshops led by visiting or resident practitioners.
